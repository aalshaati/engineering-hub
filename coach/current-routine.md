# Current Routine

The target training pattern. Unlike `principles.md` and `training.md` (stable
rules), this file is *expected to change* — revisit and tweak it at check-ins
as the program, phase, or consistency shifts.

**Program:** Push / Pull / Legs — started 2026-06-02. This is the PPL baseline
that `training.md` Rule 6 refers to.
**Phase:** Fat loss / calorie deficit. Recovery is the constraint here, not
intensity.

---

## What the evidence supports

Start from what training science says is optimal for a trained lifter, then
adjust for the deficit:

- **Frequency: ~2x per muscle group per week.** For a trained lifter, hitting
  each muscle roughly twice a week beats once a week when total volume is
  matched — it spreads the work across fresher sessions so quality per set
  stays higher. (Schoenfeld et al. frequency meta-analyses.)
- **Maintaining muscle costs far less than building it.** Minimum effective
  volume to *preserve* muscle is a fraction of what's needed to *grow* it. In
  a deficit the goal is preservation, not growth (`training.md` Rule 4), so
  the volume and frequency you actually need drops below a bulk's optimum.
- **Recovery is more expensive in a deficit.** Less fuel means slower recovery
  between sessions, so a frequency that's easy in a surplus is harder now.
  That caps how high it's worth pushing.

**Net target:** aim to train each muscle group about **every 5 days — ~4
rolling PPL sessions in a 7-day window**, with 3 as a solid floor. That nudges
toward the 2x/week optimum without the recovery cost of a full 6-day PPL,
which the deficit math doesn't favor — extra volume is better saved for diet
breaks and the eventual surplus (`training.md` Rule 5).

## Target pattern — rolling Push / Pull / Legs

Run PPL as a **continuous rolling cycle, not pinned to weekdays.** Complete a
Push, then Pull, then Legs — roughly a day between each — then start over at
Push. Over any 7-day window that lands around 3–4 sessions; aim for 4 when
recovery is good, treat 3 as the floor. The day of the week doesn't matter;
the *order* does. Walks and variable cardio fill the gaps.

**Anchor lifts per day** — the key compounds the coach tracks for progression
(`training.md` Rule 1):
- **Push** — Bench Press (barbell)
- **Pull** — Pull-Up + Lat Pulldown
- **Legs** — Squat (barbell) + Romanian Deadlift

## Feasibility check — evidence vs. actual

Recent logged training has run **~2–3 sessions/week, with one fully missed
week** (late May). That's roughly each muscle group **once** a week — under
the ~2x optimum above.

- **The gap:** evidence points to ~4 sessions/week (each muscle ~2x); you've
  been doing ~2–3 (each muscle ~1x).
- **What it means:** current frequency is *enough to maintain* with solid
  intensity and protein (`training.md` Rule 4) — it isn't a problem to fix
  urgently. But it sits at the low end, so the cheapest win is **adding one
  session to move 3 → 4**, closing most of the gap without a program overhaul.
  Not a failure to correct — just the lever with the most upside.

---

## Flexibility — this is a target, not a contract

- **The rotation rolls; the calendar doesn't.** Miss a day? Resume where you
  left off (…Push, Pull, Legs, Push…). Don't cram two sessions together or
  skip a slot to "catch up."
- **A 2-session week is fine, not a failure** — especially after heavy cardio
  or poor sleep. In a deficit, under-reaching beats digging a recovery hole.
- Variable cardio (climbing, pickleball, swimming, running) is welcome but
  counts as **recovery load**. If it's stacking up, drop a lifting session —
  don't add one.
